## 5. Percentage Calculation

### Problem Statement
A measurement of time is recorded as $t = 5.45 \pm 0.22\text{ seconds}$. What is the percentage uncertainty of this measurement?

---

### Solution

#### 1. Given Data
* **Measured Time ($t$):** $5.45\text{ s}$
* **Absolute Uncertainty ($\Delta t$):** $0.22\text{ s}$

---

#### 2. Formula for Percentage Uncertainty
Percentage uncertainty describes the error as a fraction of the total nominal measurement, scaled to a percentage format. The formula is:
$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{\Delta t}{t} \right) \times 100\%$$

---

#### 3. Calculation
Substitute the given values into the formula:
$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{0.22\text{ s}}{5.45\text{ s}} \right) \times 100\%$$

First, calculate the relative (fractional) uncertainty:
$$\frac{0.22}{5.45} \approx 0.040367$$

Next, multiply by 100 to convert to a percentage:
$$0.040367 \times 100\% \approx 4.0367\%$$

---

#### 4. Formatting and Significant Figures
* The measured value ($5.45$) has three significant figures, and the absolute uncertainty ($0.22$) has two significant figures. 
* By standard laboratory convention, percentage uncertainties are typically reported to either one or two significant figures to avoid overstating the precision of the error boundary. 
* Rounding $4.0367\%$ to two significant figures (to match the absolute uncertainty's precision) gives $4.0\%$.

### Final Answer
The percentage uncertainty of the time measurement is **$4.0\%$**.
