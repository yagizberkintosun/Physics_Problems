## Problem 1: Rotational Velocity

### Given / Knowns
* Radius of the Earth at the equator, $R = 6378 \text{ km}$
* Time for one full rotation, $T = 24 \text{ hours}$

### Concepts & Assumptions
* **Uniform Circular Motion:** A point on the Earth's equator travels in a continuous circular path. The linear speed ($v$) is the total distance traveled (the circumference of the Earth) divided by the time it takes to complete one full rotation (the period).
* **Assumption on Time:** We are assuming a standard solar day of exactly 24 hours. (Note: For absolute precision, Earth's actual rotation period relative to the fixed stars—a *sidereal day*—is roughly 23 hours, 56 minutes, and 4 seconds, or 86,164 seconds. However, 24 hours is the standard expectation for introductory mechanics unless specified otherwise).

### Formulas
* Circumference of a circle: $C = 2\pi R$
* Linear speed: $v = \frac{C}{T} = \frac{2\pi R}{T}$

### Step-by-Step Derivation
First, we need to convert the period $T$ from hours to seconds to match the requested final unit of km/s.

$$T = 24 \text{ h} \times 60 \frac{\text{min}}{\text{h}} \times 60 \frac{\text{s}}{\text{min}}$$
$$T = 86400 \text{ s}$$

Now, substitute the known values into the linear speed formula:

$$v = \frac{2 \pi R}{T}$$
$$v = \frac{2 \pi (6378 \text{ km})}{86400 \text{ s}}$$
$$v = \frac{40074.156 \text{ km}}{86400 \text{ s}}$$
$$v \approx 0.4638 \text{ km/s}$$

### Final Answer
The linear speed of a point on the Earth's equator due to its rotation is approximately **0.464 km/s**.
