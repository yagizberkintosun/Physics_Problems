## Problem 8: Interplanetary Travel

### Given / Knowns
* Distance to Mars at closest approach, $d = 55,000,000 \text{ km}$
* Speed of light, $c \approx 300,000 \text{ km/s}$
* Constant speed of spacecraft, $v_s = 40,000 \text{ km/h}$
* Constant speed of airplane, $v_a = 900 \text{ km/h}$

### Concepts & Assumptions
* **Constant Velocity Kinematics:** Since the problem states constant speeds, we can use the fundamental kinematic equation relating time, distance, and constant velocity.
* **Assumption of Linear Travel:** We are assuming the objects travel in a perfectly straight line from Earth to Mars at their closest approach. (Note: In reality, a spacecraft cannot travel in a straight line between planets; it must follow curved paths dictated by orbital mechanics, such as a Hohmann transfer orbit, which takes much longer and requires changing speeds).

### Formulas
* Time: $t = \frac{d}{v}$

### Step-by-Step Derivation

**Part a) For a message sent at the speed of light**
Using the speed of light ($c$):
$$t_{light} = \frac{d}{c}$$
$$t_{light} = \frac{55,000,000 \text{ km}}{300,000 \text{ km/s}}$$
$$t_{light} \approx 183.33 \text{ s}$$
To make this more intuitive, convert it to minutes:
$$183.33 \text{ s} \times \left(\frac{1 \text{ min}}{60 \text{ s}}\right) \approx 3.06 \text{ minutes}$$

**Part b) By a spacecraft traveling at a constant speed of 40,000 km/h**
Using the spacecraft's speed in km/h is easier here so we can find the time in hours:
$$t_{spacecraft} = \frac{d}{v_s}$$
$$t_{spacecraft} = \frac{55,000,000 \text{ km}}{40,000 \text{ km/h}}$$
$$t_{spacecraft} = 1375 \text{ hours}$$
Convert hours to days for a more standard interplanetary timeframe:
$$1375 \text{ hours} \times \left(\frac{1 \text{ day}}{24 \text{ hours}}\right) \approx 57.29 \text{ days}$$

**Part c) By "airplane" traveling at a constant speed of 900 km/h**
Using the airplane's speed:
$$t_{airplane} = \frac{d}{v_a}$$
$$t_{airplane} = \frac{55,000,000 \text{ km}}{900 \text{ km/h}}$$
$$t_{airplane} \approx 61,111.11 \text{ hours}$$
Convert hours to days, and then to years:
$$61,111.11 \text{ hours} \times \left(\frac{1 \text{ day}}{24 \text{ hours}}\right) \approx 2546.3 \text{ days}$$
$$2546.3 \text{ days} \times \left(\frac{1 \text{ year}}{365.25 \text{ days}}\right) \approx 6.97 \text{ years}$$

### Final Answer
* **a)** A light-speed message would take approximately **183.3 seconds** (about **3.06 minutes**).
* **b)** A spacecraft at 40,000 km/h would take **1375 hours** (about **57.3 days**).
* **c)** An airplane at 900 km/h would take roughly **61,111 hours** (nearly **7 years**).
